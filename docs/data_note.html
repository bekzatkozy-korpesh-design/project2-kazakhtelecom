Data note: fixed internet speed tiles in Kazakhstan, Q3 2025 and Q3 2026

Files: data_raw.csv (194,851 tiles) → data_clean.csv (88,242 rows × 17 columns). One row = one ~610 m map tile, one network (fixed or mobile), one quarter. Bekzat Kozy-Korpesh, 05-P.

Where the data comes from (all real, downloaded 2026-11-25)

  • Ookla Speedtest Open Data, fixed network performance tiles, licence CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Files: https://ookla-open-data.s3.amazonaws.com/parquet/performance/type=fixed/year=2025/quarter=3/2025-07-01_performance_fixed_tiles.parquet, the same path for fixed year=2026, and type=mobile year=2026 (…_performance_mobile_tiles.parquet). Each tile holds the average of all Speedtest tests run in that ~610 m square during the quarter.
  • geoBoundaries KAZ ADM1 (regions), CC BY 4.0: https://github.com/wmgeolab/geoBoundaries (gbOpen/KAZ/ADM1).
  • GeoNames KZ (settlements with population), CC BY 4.0: https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/KZ.zip.
  • Kazakh sources for the problem: ranking.kz, zakon.kz, inform.kz, digitalbusiness.kz (links in hypotheses.md).

How raw was made: extract_raw.py keeps every tile whose centre falls in Kazakhstan's bounding box (lon 46.4–87.4, lat 40.5–55.5). Nothing else is changed.

How raw became clean (analysis.ipynb, logged in cleaning_log.csv)

  • Duplicates: none found (quadkey + network + quarter is unique).
  • Outside Kazakhstan: 106,609 tiles dropped. The bounding box also covers parts of Russia, China, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan and Turkmenistan; a point-in-polygon test against the 16 regions keeps only Kazakh tiles.
  • Units: kbps → Mbps for both speeds; latency stays in ms.
  • Impossible values: tiles with 0 Mbps or 0 tests are dropped; none were found.
  • Outliers, checked and kept: the 1% / 50% / 99% / max download speeds are 0.8 / 68.9 / 520 / 1,882 Mbps, and 31 tiles have latency above 1,000 ms. These are real tests (gigabit fibre exists; satellite gives high latency). The tests use log speed and medians, so a few extreme tiles cannot drive the result.
  • Missing values: none in the source columns.
  • Added columns: region, settlement class, nearest town and the distances to it, a date, and slow_25.

Settlement rule (fixed before the data was opened): city = a GeoNames place with ≥ 100,000 people within 15 km (23 such places); village = no place with ≥ 10,000 people within 15 km; town = everything else.

What is missing: bias check

Who is missing. Only people who chose to run a Speedtest appear, and people often test when the line feels slow. That can pull speeds down everywhere, probably more in villages where problems are common. Places with no fixed internet at all leave no tiles, so the worst villages are invisible: the true rural gap is likely larger than measured.

Uneven coverage. City tiles carry far more tests (233,290 fixed in Q3 2026) than village tiles (49,708). Mobile has fewer tests overall (172,193), and only tiles with both a fixed and a mobile test can be compared (12,580 city or village tiles). Mobile tests in a village tile may come from people passing through.

Period. Only two summer quarters are covered, one year apart. The jump from 15 to 43 Mbps in villages may partly reflect who tested, not only new lines. The paired check on the same 4,180 tiles (21 → 43 Mbps) reduces this risk but does not remove it.

Provider unknown. Ookla does not name the operator, so the link to Kazakhtelecom rests on its ~66% share of fixed broadband.

Maps. The region borders are pre-2022 (no Abai, Zhetysu or Ulytau regions; Shymkent sits inside South Kazakhstan). GeoNames population figures can be out of date.

What it must not be used for

Not for judging single households, streets or named villages: one tile averages many lines. Not for ranking providers, because the provider is not in the data. Not for commercial use (the Ookla licence is non-commercial). Not as official statistics of Kazakhtelecom or the Ministry.
